Brief for legal advice: holding customer payments on a service marketplace

From: Piron Work, Phnom Penh, [owner name, phone, email] · Date: 15 October 2026 · Product: https://app.piron.tech

1. The business

Piron Work is a web application where customers in Cambodia post small service projects (design, bookkeeping, documents, repairs, lessons) and independent providers send quotes. Providers pass an identity check (ID document and selfie, reviewed by a person). The pilot is in Phnom Penh. Today the customer and provider agree a price and pay each other directly. The app only keeps invoices and payment records and shows who marked what as paid. It never receives or sends money. Legal entity: [registered company, type and number, or not yet registered].

2. The model we are considering

  1. The customer sends the agreed price to a bank account named for Piron Work (or for a licensed partner) by bank transfer, ABA, Wing or KHQR.
  2. Our staff confirm the money arrived. The job shows the payment as held and the provider starts work.
  3. The customer confirms the work and the payment is released. If the customer does not answer within a set number of days after delivery it is released automatically. In a dispute the money stays held and staff decide release or refund.
  4. We pay the provider the amount minus a platform fee (for example 5%) to an account whose holder name matches the provider's verified ID.

Expected size: small jobs, [typical amount and expected monthly volume]. This flow is designed but not built and not switched on. We will not enable it before advice.

3. Our preliminary reading (from secondary sources, not verified)

  • Providing means of payment and taking deposits from the public are licensed banking activities under the Law on Banking and Financial Institutions (1999); doing even one counts as banking.
  • The 2017 National Bank of Cambodia Prakas on payment service institutions bars unlicensed entities from issuing electronic money; a licence reportedly needs substantial capital and segregated customer funds.
  • A National Bank notification (No. 14.026.1075, 6 August 2026) asks non-bank issuers of single-purpose e-money to notify by 4 November 2026.
  • We found nothing that addresses a marketplace holding funds until work is confirmed. We also note the E-commerce Law and Consumer Protection Law of 2019.

4. Questions

  1. Does the model in section 2 need a National Bank licence, or is it a regulated deposit or payment service? Under which provisions?
  2. Is there an exemption, agency or notification route for a marketplace that holds funds only for the length of a job?
  3. If a licence is needed: which type, minimum capital and timeline? Can we avoid it by using a licensed bank or payment institution that collects and pays out, with Piron Work only instructing? What contract structure is acceptable?
  4. Is the current model acceptable: direct payment, with invoices, "marked as paid" and "payment received" records kept in the app?
  5. Tax: must invoices or receipts follow a General Department of Taxation format; do we have VAT, withholding or reporting duties on provider payouts or on our fee; what registration is needed first?
  6. Anti-money-laundering and identity duties if we facilitate payments, and how long we must keep ID and bank data.
  7. Consumer protection, refund and dispute duties, and the terms and disclosures we should publish.
  8. What entity and registration must exist before any payment is handled?
